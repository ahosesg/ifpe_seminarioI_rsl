\noindent This systematized review assessed the comparability of conventional automatic and gravimetric methods for ambient PM10 and PM2.5. Searches in Scopus, Web of Science, and PubMed, complemented by a bounded citation search, yielded 437 unique records. Among 84 reports assessed, 66 were selected, covering publications from 1991 to 2025. Focused extraction and descriptive critical analysis distinguished particle fractions, instrument configurations, comparators, denominators, and statistical definitions, without meta-analysis. Differences varied with thermal treatment, preservation of semivolatile material, particle-associated water, and aerosol properties. Systems compensating for semivolatile losses could exceed reference filters affected by volatilization. Corrections improved results in specific datasets, with their scope constrained by the validation design. High correlation did not establish agreement, and the reported indicators did not constitute a common performance scale. The findings support evaluating the complete measurement system and documenting instrument transitions before combining time series or assessing temporal representativeness.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} particulate matter; gravimetry; continuous monitoring; comparability; relative humidity.\par
